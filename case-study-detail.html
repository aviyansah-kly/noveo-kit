Case Study · SaaS

Extending runway without putting growth on hold.

A founder-led subscription business needed a more reliable way to connect retention, hiring, acquisition spend, and cash before committing to its next stage of growth.

The context

Growth was working. The planning model was not.

The company had recurring revenue, a healthy product, and an ambitious hiring plan. But its financial view was split across bookkeeping reports, a sales forecast, a hiring sheet, and founder-maintained cash assumptions.

Leadership could see the past, but not the tradeoffs ahead.

The main question was not whether the business could grow. It was how quickly it could add people and acquisition spend without forcing an early financing decision.

The challenge

Four moving parts were changing the runway at once.

Instead of treating cash as a single output, NOVEO rebuilt the model around the operating drivers behind it.

Retention

Small churn changes had a large cash effect.

The old plan used one retention assumption without showing how downside scenarios changed hiring capacity.

Hiring

Roles were approved without timing scenarios.

Leadership needed to compare the impact of hiring now, delaying, or sequencing roles differently.

Acquisition

Growth spend was disconnected from payback.

Marketing budgets were not consistently tied to CAC, payback, gross margin, and runway.

Capital

Fundraising timing was becoming reactive.

The team wanted to understand when capital would be optional, helpful, or necessary under each scenario.

What we changed

One operating model for growth, cash, and decisions.

The work focused on a small number of tools that leadership could use repeatedly.

01

Integrated forecast

Revenue, churn, hiring, acquisition spend, gross margin, and cash were connected in one forward model.

02

Scenario planning

Base, conservative, and accelerated growth paths made the runway tradeoffs visible before commitments were made.

03

Leadership dashboard

A concise KPI view connected forecast assumptions to actual performance and highlighted where the plan was drifting.

04

Weekly finance cadence

Finance moved into the operating rhythm so changes in retention, hiring, or spend could be acted on earlier.

Outcome

More runway came from better sequencing, not a growth freeze.

The company changed when and where it committed cash instead of applying an across-the-board cut.

Runway

2.4× longer modeled runway.

By sequencing hires and acquisition spend against realistic retention and payback assumptions.

Forecasting

96% forecast accuracy.

Improved assumption ownership and regular actual-vs-plan review made the model more dependable over time.

Decision speed

Hiring discussions became faster.

Leadership could compare the cash and runway impact of each role before approval.

What changed operationally

The biggest improvement was confidence in the next decision.

The team no longer had to rebuild financial context every time the growth plan changed. The model became a shared reference point for finance, hiring, marketing, and capital discussions.

Finance moved from explanation to anticipation.

Instead of asking what happened after month-end, leadership could ask what would happen if they changed the plan now.
